\label{sec:eval}

\subsection{Single actions}
In the default cell, delay-only costs \$\DefDelayUsdZero{} per MWh at $w_f=0$ and \$\DefDelayUsdOne{} at $w_f=1$. It is the cheapest of the three single actions.
Cap-only cannot meet the target. Even at the deepest cap level it sheds \DefCapSharePct\% of the target in the default cell, and it is non-compliant in \CapNoncompliantCells{} of the \CellsTotal{} cells.
Burst-only complies but is expensive. In the default cell its per-event cost is a median of \BurstOverDelayDefaultOne{} ($w_f=1$) to \BurstOverDelayDefaultZero{} ($w_f=0$) times that of delay-only; over all \SetupEventsFeasible{} events the medians are \BurstOverDelayAllOne{} ($w_f=1$) and \BurstOverDelayAllZero{} ($w_f=0$) times.
The reason is that the cloud bill covers each burst job's whole remaining runtime, while the grid values only the hours of the event.

\subsection{The mix controller}
\label{sec:mixctl}
With the 90th-percentile cloud bill, the mix is cheaper than delay-only with each of the three learned predictors. With the transformer, its per-event cost ratio to delay-only has a median of \MixPNineOverDelayZero{} at $w_f=0$ and \MixPNineOverDelayOne{} at $w_f=1$, and it is cheaper in \MixPNineOverDelayCheaperZero{} and \MixPNineOverDelayCheaperOne{} of the \SetupEventsFeasible{} events. With gradient-boosted trees the median ratio is \SetGbdtMainMixOverDelayZero{} and \SetGbdtMainMixOverDelayOne{} (cheaper in \SetGbdtMainCheaperZero{} and \SetGbdtMainCheaperOne{} events), and with LightGBM \SetLightgbmMainMixOverDelayZero{} and \SetLightgbmMainMixOverDelayOne{} (\SetLightgbmMainCheaperZero{} and \SetLightgbmMainCheaperOne{} events). The per-event gain at $w_f=0$ is therefore \LearnedGainMinPct{} to \LearnedGainMaxPct\%, depending on the predictor; the tree models gain more than the transformer.
Resampling the \BootDays{} event days (\BootResamples{} resamples) gives 95\% confidence intervals for the median ratio of \BootTransLoZero{}--\BootTransHiZero{} (transformer), \BootGbdtLoZero{}--\BootGbdtHiZero{} (gradient-boosted trees) and \BootLightgbmLoZero{}--\BootLightgbmHiZero{} (LightGBM) at $w_f=0$, and \BootTransLoOne{}--\BootTransHiOne{}, \BootGbdtLoOne{}--\BootGbdtHiOne{} and \BootLightgbmLoOne{}--\BootLightgbmHiOne{} at $w_f=1$; all lie below 1.
Summed over all events, the transformer mix costs \SetTransMainReductionPctZero\% ($w_f=0$) and \SetTransMainReductionPctOne\% ($w_f=1$) less than delay-only; for the tree models the reductions are \SetGbdtMainReductionPctZero\% and \SetGbdtMainReductionPctOne\% (gradient-boosted trees) and \SetLightgbmMainReductionPctZero\% and \SetLightgbmMainReductionPctOne\% (LightGBM). Per-event medians, total-cost reductions and the default-cell \$/MWh of Table~\ref{tab:main} are different statistics and need not agree.
With the median cloud bill, the mix does not help: its median ratio is \MixOverDelayZero{} at $w_f=0$, and it is cheaper in only \MixOverDelayCheaperZero{} events. With the last-two-jobs predictor the mix costs \MixLastTwoOverDelayZero{} times as much as delay-only at $w_f=0$. This predictor gives a job's remaining runtime from the user's last two runtimes, so a job that has already run longer sits at the 60\,s floor: in the default cell \LastTwoHoldBurstFloorPct\% of the jobs its mix bursts are at the floor (against \LastTwoHoldRunningFloorPct\% of all running jobs), and their actual remaining runtime has a median of \LastTwoHoldBurstActualRemH{} hours.

\subsection{Where the gain comes from}
Mix-nocloud runs the same controller with no cloud capacity. With the p90 bill, the median ratio of mix to mix-nocloud is \LearnedMixOverNocloudMax{} for all three learned predictors at both cost weightings. The gain over delay-only therefore comes from capping and from choosing which jobs to suspend, not from bursting: with the transformer, mix-nocloud alone has a median ratio to delay-only of \NocloudOverDelayZero{} ($w_f=0$) and \NocloudOverDelayOne{} ($w_f=1$).
In our runs, bursting helped only with the oracle's exact runtimes. With the oracle, the median ratio of mix to mix-nocloud is \MixOverNocloudOracleZero{} over all events at $w_f=0$, and \MixOverNocloudOracleFourThirtyZero{} and \MixOverNocloudOracleFourTwentyZero{} in the 4\,h cells with 30\% and 20\% cuts. At $w_f=1$ it is \MixOverNocloudOracleOne{} overall and \MixOverNocloudOracleFourThirtyOne{} at 4\,h and 30\%.
The cloud limit of \SetupCloudCapGpus{} GPUs also matters: burst-only complies in \BurstThirtyCompliantEvents{} of the \BurstThirtyEvents{} events with a 30\% cut. In a diagnostic run on \DiagCapThreeKEvents{} such events (last-two-jobs predictor, contract rule) with 3,000 cloud GPUs, burst-only complied in \DiagCapThreeKCompliantEvents{}, at a cloud bill of \$\DiagCapThreeKCloudKusdMin{}k to \$\DiagCapThreeKCloudKusdMax{}k per event.

\subsection{Predictions for long-running jobs}
How a prediction is read beyond the last grid point decides the result. Under the contract rule, the transformer mix costs \ContractOverHoldMin{} to \ContractOverHoldMax{} times as much per event as under the hold-beyond-grid rule, depending on the cloud-bill quantile and $w_f$ (Fig.~\ref{fig:selectors}).
The cause is the running population at an event: \MotRunningBeyondGridJobsPct\% of the running jobs have run longer than 12\,h. Under the contract rule their predicted remaining runtime falls to the 60\,s floor. In the default cell, \ContractBurstFloorMinPct\% to \ContractBurstFloorMaxPct\% of the jobs that the mix bursts are at this floor, against \ContractRunningFloorMinPct\% to \ContractRunningFloorMaxPct\% of all running jobs. Their actual remaining runtime has a median of \ContractBurstActualRemMinH{} to \ContractBurstActualRemMaxH{} hours, so the cloud bills many hours that the controller did not expect.

\subsection{Prediction accuracy and decision cost}
The learned predictors differ little in accuracy; in the default cell they also differ little in decision cost, and the large gaps are to last-two-jobs and to the oracle. On the test split, gradient-boosted trees have the lowest pinball loss (\PredGbdtPinball{}, against \PredLightgbmPinball{} for LightGBM, \PredTransformerPinball{} for the transformer and \PredLastTwoPinball{} for last-two-jobs). The transformer has the highest AUROC for Failed jobs (\PredTransformerAuroc{}, against \PredLightgbmAuroc{}, \PredGbdtAuroc{} and \PredLastTwoAuroc{}).
In the default cell (median cloud bill), the median mix cost per event is \$\EthreeTransformerMixUsdEvent{}, \$\EthreeGbdtMixUsdEvent{} and \$\EthreeLightgbmMixUsdEvent{} with the transformer, gradient-boosted trees and LightGBM, \$\EthreeLastTwoMixUsdEvent{} with last-two-jobs and \$\EthreeOracleMixUsdEvent{} with the oracle.
Over all events the ranking changes: the p90 mix gains more with the tree models than with the transformer (Section~\ref{sec:mixctl}). This holds even against the transformer's delay-only, where the two tree models reach median ratios of \LearnedGbdtMixOverTransDelayZero{} and \LearnedLightgbmMixOverTransDelayZero{} at $w_f=0$. The model with the lowest pinball loss, gradient-boosted trees, also had the largest gain; the transformer's higher AUROC did not give a larger gain.
A delay-only controller with no predictor (every job counted as useful) shows what the predictions buy. At $w_f=0$ it costs \BlindOverTransDelayZero{} times as much as delay-only with the transformer, and the p90 mixes cost \TransMixOverBlindZero{} (transformer), \GbdtMixOverBlindZero{} (gradient-boosted trees) and \LightgbmMixOverBlindZero{} (LightGBM) of it. At $w_f=1$ it is cheaper than delay-only with any predictor, including the oracle (\BlindOverOracleDelayOne{}), and the p90 mixes cost \TransMixOverBlindOne{}, \GbdtMixOverBlindOne{} and \LightgbmMixOverBlindOne{} of it: the predictions steer delay onto jobs likely to fail, which pays off only when such delay is free.

\subsection{Robustness}
\emph{Cost weighting.} The hold estimate (Eq.~\ref{eq:hold}) does not use $w_f$, so the controller takes the same actions at both weightings; only the accounting changes. At $w_f=1$ the ranking of the single actions and the gain of the p90 mix over delay-only with the same predictor are the same (Table~\ref{tab:main}); the median-bill mix reaches a ratio of \MixOverDelayOne{} there.
\emph{Model parameters.} Table~\ref{tab:sens} varies one parameter at a time in the default cell. Most leave the median ratio of the p90 mix to delay-only near its default of \SensDefaultRatio{}. Host power matters: at half and 1.5 times our assumed host power the ratio is \SensHostHalfRatio{} and \SensHostOneHalfRatio{}. With the pessimistic cap curve the gain disappears (ratio \SensPessimisticRatio{}, cheaper in \SensPessimisticCheaper{} of \SensEvents{} events), so the gain holds only under our default cap curve.

\emph{Cloud price.} In the default cell, burst-only costs a median of \PriceSpotBurstOverDelay{}, \PriceLowBurstOverDelay{}, \PriceBaseBurstOverDelay{} and \PriceDoubleBurstOverDelay{} times as much per MWh as delay-only at \$\PriceSpotUsd{}, \$\PriceLowUsd{}, \$\PriceBaseUsd{} and \$\PriceDoubleUsd{} per GPU-hour; even the spot price leaves bursting far behind. With the oracle, a lower price helps the mix: its ratio to the oracle's delay-only is \PriceSpotOracleMixOverDelay{} at the spot price (cheaper in \PriceSpotOracleMixCheaper{} of \SensEvents{} events) against \PriceBaseOracleMixOverDelay{} at the base price.
\emph{Lookup rule.} The contract rule changes the learned-predictor results (above), not the oracle's.
\emph{Clean jobs.} If only jobs submitted in the test window may be burst, so that every burst decision uses out-of-sample predictions, the median ratio of the p90 mix to delay-only in the default cell is \OosMixPNineOverDelayZero{} ($w_f=0$), as in the main run (\DefMixPNineOverDelayZero{}).
\emph{Jobs without predictions.} If these jobs get fixed uninformed values (30 days of remaining runtime, no failure risk) instead of their true values, the median ratio of the p90 mix to delay-only is \RobustUninfMixOverDelayZero{} ($w_f=0$) and \RobustUninfMixOverDelayOne{} ($w_f=1$), unchanged. These jobs are censored, treated as Pass and have hundreds of hours left, so both fallbacks lead to the same decisions.
\emph{In-sample jobs.} Jobs submitted before the validation period were in the learned models' training set; at an event start they are a median of \InsampleRunningJobsPct\% of the running jobs and \InsampleRunningGpusPct\% of the running GPUs. If the learned predictors give these jobs the same uninformed values, the median ratio of the p90 mix to delay-only is \SetTransMaskedMixOverDelayZero{} with the transformer, \SetGbdtMaskedMixOverDelayZero{} with gradient-boosted trees and \SetLightgbmMaskedMixOverDelayZero{} with LightGBM at $w_f=0$ (\SetTransMaskedMixOverDelayOne{}, \SetGbdtMaskedMixOverDelayOne{} and \SetLightgbmMaskedMixOverDelayOne{} at $w_f=1$). No predictor's ratio moves by more than \LearnedMaskShiftMax{}.
