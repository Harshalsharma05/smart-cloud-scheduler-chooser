\subsection{Single actions}
\label{sec:single}
In the default cell, delay-only costs \$\DefDelayUsdZero{} per MWh at $w_f=0$ and \$\DefDelayUsdOne{} at $w_f=1$. It is the cheapest of the three single actions.
Cap-only cannot meet the target. Even at the deepest cap level it sheds \DefCapSharePct\% of the target in the default cell, and it is non-compliant in \CapNoncompliantCells{} of the \CellsTotal{} cells.
Burst-only complies but is expensive. In the default cell its per-event cost is a median of \BurstOverDelayDefaultOne{} ($w_f=1$) to \BurstOverDelayDefaultZero{} ($w_f=0$) times that of delay-only; over all \SetupEventsFeasible{} events the medians are \BurstOverDelayAllOne{} ($w_f=1$) and \BurstOverDelayAllZero{} ($w_f=0$) times, and it is cheaper than delay-only in \BurstCheaperThanDelayZero{} events at either weighting.

\subsection{The mix controller}
\label{sec:mixctl}
With the 90th-percentile cloud bill, the mix is cheaper than delay-only with each of the three learned predictors. With the transformer, its per-event cost ratio to delay-only has a median of \MixPNineOverDelayZero{} at $w_f=0$ and \MixPNineOverDelayOne{} at $w_f=1$, and it is cheaper in \MixPNineOverDelayCheaperZero{} and \MixPNineOverDelayCheaperOne{} of the \SetupEventsFeasible{} events. With gradient-boosted trees the median ratio is \SetGbdtMainMixOverDelayZero{} and \SetGbdtMainMixOverDelayOne{} (cheaper in \SetGbdtMainCheaperZero{} and \SetGbdtMainCheaperOne{} events), and with LightGBM \SetLightgbmMainMixOverDelayZero{} and \SetLightgbmMainMixOverDelayOne{} (\SetLightgbmMainCheaperZero{} and \SetLightgbmMainCheaperOne{} events). The per-event gain at $w_f=0$ is therefore \LearnedGainMinPct{} to \LearnedGainMaxPct\%, depending on the predictor; the tree models gain more than the transformer.
Resampling the \BootDays{} event days gives 95\% confidence intervals for the median ratio that lie below 1 for all three predictors at both weightings (transformer: \BootTransLoZero{}--\BootTransHiZero{} at $w_f=0$).
Summed over all events, the transformer mix costs \SetTransMainReductionPctZero\% ($w_f=0$) and \SetTransMainReductionPctOne\% ($w_f=1$) less than delay-only; for the tree models the reductions are \SetGbdtMainReductionPctZero\% and \SetGbdtMainReductionPctOne\% (gradient-boosted trees) and \SetLightgbmMainReductionPctZero\% and \SetLightgbmMainReductionPctOne\% (LightGBM). With the median cloud bill, the mix does not help: its median ratio is \MixOverDelayZero{} at $w_f=0$, and it is cheaper in only \MixOverDelayCheaperZero{} events. With the last-two-jobs predictor the mix costs \MixLastTwoOverDelayZero{} times as much as delay-only at $w_f=0$: a job that has run longer than the user's last two jobs sits at the 60\,s floor, so the mix bursts long jobs.

\subsection{Where the gain comes from}
Mix-nocloud runs the same controller with no cloud capacity. With the p90 bill, the median ratio of mix to mix-nocloud is \LearnedMixOverNocloudMax{} for all three learned predictors at both cost weightings. The gain over delay-only therefore comes from capping and from choosing which jobs to suspend, not from bursting: with the transformer, mix-nocloud alone has a median ratio to delay-only of \NocloudOverDelayZero{} ($w_f=0$) and \NocloudOverDelayOne{} ($w_f=1$).
In our runs, bursting helped only with the oracle's exact runtimes. With the oracle, the median ratio of mix to mix-nocloud is \MixOverNocloudOracleZero{} over all events at $w_f=0$, and \MixOverNocloudOracleFourThirtyZero{} and \MixOverNocloudOracleFourTwentyZero{} in the 4\,h cells with 30\% and 20\% cuts.
The cloud limit of \SetupCloudCapGpus{} GPUs also matters: burst-only complies in \BurstThirtyCompliantEvents{} of the \BurstThirtyEvents{} events with a 30\% cut.

\subsection{Predictions for long-running jobs}
How a prediction is read beyond the last grid point determines the result. Under the contract rule, the transformer mix costs \ContractOverHoldMin{} to \ContractOverHoldMax{} times as much per event as under the hold-beyond-grid rule, depending on the cloud-bill quantile and $w_f$ (Fig.~\ref{fig:selectors}).
The cause is the running population at an event: \MotRunningBeyondGridJobsPct\% of the running jobs have run longer than 12\,h. Under the contract rule their predicted remaining runtime falls to the 60\,s floor. In the default cell, \ContractBurstFloorMinPct\% to \ContractBurstFloorMaxPct\% of the jobs that the mix bursts are at this floor, against \ContractRunningFloorMinPct\% to \ContractRunningFloorMaxPct\% of all running jobs. Their actual remaining runtime has a median of \ContractBurstActualRemMinH{} to \ContractBurstActualRemMaxH{} hours, so the cloud bills many hours that the controller did not anticipate.

\subsection{Prediction accuracy and decision cost}
\label{sec:accuracy}
On the test split, gradient-boosted trees have the lowest pinball loss (\PredGbdtPinball{}, against \PredLightgbmPinball{} for LightGBM, \PredTransformerPinball{} for the transformer and \PredLastTwoPinball{} for last-two-jobs). The transformer has the highest AUROC for Failed jobs (\PredTransformerAuroc{}, against \PredLightgbmAuroc{}, \PredGbdtAuroc{} and \PredLastTwoAuroc{}).
The model with the lowest pinball loss, gradient-boosted trees, also had the largest gain (Section~\ref{sec:mixctl}); the transformer's higher AUROC did not give a larger gain.
A delay-only controller with no predictor (every job counted as useful) shows what the predictions buy: at $w_f=0$ it costs \BlindOverTransDelayZero{} times as much as delay-only with the transformer, but at $w_f=1$ it is cheaper than delay-only with any predictor, including the oracle (\BlindOverOracleDelayOne{}). The predictions direct delay toward jobs likely to fail, which pays off only when such delay is free.

Delay-only is again the cheapest action that meets the cut: \$\AcmeDefDelayUsdZero{} per MWh in the default cell at $w_f=0$ and \$\AcmeDefDelayUsdOne{} at $w_f=1$. With the learned predictors it is cheaper than delay-only in \AcmeLearnedBurstCheaperMaxZero{} events at $w_f=0$ and at most \AcmeLearnedBurstCheaperMaxOne{} at $w_f=1$; with the oracle's exact runtimes, bursting beats delay in \AcmeOracleBurstCheaperZero{} of the \AcmeEvents{} events (\AcmeOracleBurstCheaperOne{} at $w_f=1$). A cheaper cloud does not change this: at \$\AcmeCheapPrice{} per GPU-hour burst-only still costs \AcmeCheapBurstTransformerOverDelayZero{} (transformer) and \AcmeCheapBurstOracleOverDelayZero{} (oracle) times as much as delay-only in the default cell, and at \$\AcmeHighPrice{} \AcmeHighBurstTransformerOverDelayZero{} times.
The gap is much smaller than on Philly because Acme jobs are short; its cause, however, is the same tail: jobs with more than an hour left are \AcmeBurstLongJobsPct\% of the jobs that burst-only moves with the transformer, yet \AcmeBurstLongUsdPct\% of its cloud bill.

The mix gains at most \AcmeLearnedMixGainMaxPct\% on Acme. With the p90 cloud bill its median ratio to delay-only is \AcmeLearnedMixMinZero{}--\AcmeLearnedMixMaxZero{} for the three learned predictors at $w_f=0$ (cheaper in \AcmeLearnedMixCheaperMinZero{} to \AcmeLearnedMixCheaperMaxZero{} events) and \AcmeLearnedMixMinOne{}--\AcmeLearnedMixMaxOne{} at $w_f=1$, and offloading adds nothing to it (mix-p90 to mix-nocloud: \AcmeLearnedNocloudMinZero{}--\AcmeLearnedNocloudMaxZero{}). With the median bill the mix costs \AcmeLearnedMixPFiveMinZero{} to \AcmeLearnedMixPFiveMaxZero{} times as much as delay-only: it bursts jobs predicted to end soon that then run for more than an hour. Exact runtimes would help: the oracle mix is \AcmeOracleMixGainPctZero\% cheaper than delay-only (cheaper in \AcmeMixPNineOverDelayCheaperOracleZero{} events).
The transformer is the most accurate predictor on Acme, with the highest AUROC for failed jobs (\AcmePredTransformerAuroc{}, against \AcmePredGbdtAuroc{} and \AcmePredLightgbmAuroc{} for the tree models) and a tie on pinball loss (\AcmePredTransformerPinball{}, \AcmePredGbdtPinball{} and \AcmePredLightgbmPinball{}), yet its mix is not cheaper. As on Philly, accuracy did not predict decision cost.

\subsection{Robustness}
\emph{Planning with $w_f$.} The hold estimate (Eq.~\ref{eq:hold}) does not use $w_f$. A variant that plans with $q'_j=q_j+w_f(1-q_j)$ makes the p90 mix cost a median of \PlanwfTransOverBlind{}, \PlanwfGbdtOverBlind{} and \PlanwfLightgbmOverBlind{} of the no-predictor delay-only at $w_f=1$ (transformer, gradient-boosted trees, LightGBM; cheaper in \PlanwfTransOverBlindCheaper{}, \PlanwfGbdtOverBlindCheaper{} and \PlanwfLightgbmOverBlindCheaper{} events).
\emph{Model parameters.} Table~\ref{tab:sens} varies one parameter at a time in the default cell. Only host power and the cap curve move the median ratio of the p90 mix to delay-only noticeably from its default of \SensDefaultRatio{}. With the pessimistic cap curve the gain disappears (ratio \SensPessimisticRatio{}, cheaper in \SensPessimisticCheaper{} of \SensEvents{} events), so the gain holds only under our default cap curve.

\emph{Cloud price.} In the default cell, burst-only costs a median of \PriceSpotBurstOverDelay{}, \PriceLowBurstOverDelay{}, \PriceBaseBurstOverDelay{} and \PriceDoubleBurstOverDelay{} times as much per MWh as delay-only at \$\PriceSpotUsd{}, \$\PriceLowUsd{}, \$\PriceBaseUsd{} and \$\PriceDoubleUsd{} per GPU-hour; even the spot price, which needs care with deadlines~\cite{wu24}, leaves bursting far behind. With the oracle, a lower price helps the mix: its ratio to the oracle's delay-only is \PriceSpotOracleMixOverDelay{} at the spot price (cheaper in \PriceSpotOracleMixCheaper{} of \SensEvents{} events) against \PriceBaseOracleMixOverDelay{} at the base price.
\emph{Clean jobs.} If only jobs submitted in the test window may be burst (out-of-sample predictions only), the median ratio of the p90 mix to delay-only in the default cell is \OosMixPNineOverDelayZero{}, as in the main run (\DefMixPNineOverDelayZero{}).
\emph{Jobs without predictions.} Giving them fixed uninformed values (30 days left, no failure risk) instead of their true values leaves the median ratio of the p90 mix to delay-only at \RobustUninfMixOverDelayZero{} ($w_f=0$) and \RobustUninfMixOverDelayOne{} ($w_f=1$).
\emph{In-sample jobs.} Jobs submitted before the validation period, a median of \InsampleRunningGpusPct\% of the running GPUs at an event start, are in the learned models' training set; giving them the same uninformed values moves no predictor's median ratio by more than \LearnedMaskShiftMax{}.
