\section{Discussion and Limitations}
\label{sec:discussion}

\emph{When could offloading pay?} Five conditions favour bursting; our traces show how far each holds. (i)~\emph{Jobs close to the end}: the bill covers the whole remaining runtime, so only jobs that end within about the event are cheap to move. On Acme, jobs with more than an hour left are \AcmeBurstLongJobsPct\% of the burst jobs but \AcmeBurstLongUsdPct\% of the bill, and on Philly \MotRunningBeyondGridJobsPct\% of the running jobs have run for more than 12 hours. (ii)~\emph{Costly delay}: even when delay of failed work is counted ($w_f=1$), burst-only costs \BurstOverDelayDefaultOne{} (Philly) and \AcmeBurstOverDelayDefaultOne{} (Acme) times as much as delay in the default cell. (iii)~\emph{Long, deep events}: on Philly, bursting helped only the oracle mix, mainly in the 4-hour cells with 20--30\% cuts; it also needs cloud capacity, since with \SetupCloudCapGpus{} cloud GPUs burst-only met no 30\% cut there. (iv)~\emph{Cheap cloud}: burst-only still costs \PriceSpotBurstOverDelay{} times as much as delay at the spot price on Philly, and \AcmeCheapBurstTransformerOverDelayZero{} times at \$\AcmeCheapPrice{} on Acme. (v)~\emph{Jobs that can return}: in our model a burst job finishes in the cloud. If it returned when the event ends, the bill would cover about the event plus overheads, $\pi g_j(D+\tau_b+\tau_m)/3600$, against $c_d g_j D q_j/3600$ for holding it, so bursting would win only if $c_d q_j>\pi$ by more than the overheads: when an hour of useful delay costs more than a rented GPU-hour. With our $c_d=\pi$ this never holds.

\emph{Limitations.} We use two traces, each from one organisation and with one month of test data. The simulator has no virtual-cluster quotas, placement constraints or start-up delay, so its waits differ from the traces (Section~\ref{sec:method}). The GPU pool is constant. The prediction grid ends at 12\,h. Our default plan ignores $w_f$; at $w_f=1$ a plan that uses it does better (Section~\ref{sec:eval}). The transformer was trained with one seed. On Philly, jobs submitted in the training period appear at test events; masking their predictions moved the median ratio of the p90 mix to delay-only by at most \LearnedMaskShiftMax{}. Host power on Philly, the cap curve and the migration and restore times are assumptions (Table~\ref{tab:params}). Acme has no per-job utilisation, so all its jobs share a calibrated value; we use only its Seren cluster; its job-type labels are not published (a model without them is about as accurate); and its events follow China time. Neither trace contains inference serving. The carbon check uses annual average intensities and event-window energy only. The cloud prices come from a listing site.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

Delay is the cheapest way for a GPU cluster to cut power on request, on a 2017 deep-learning trace and on a 2023 LLM-development trace. Capping alone falls short, and bursting alone costs a median of \BurstOverDelayDefaultZero{} (Philly) and \AcmeBurstOverDelayDefaultZero{} (Acme) times as much as delay per event for a 2-hour, 20\% cut, because the cloud bill covers the whole remaining runtime and a few long jobs dominate it. A controller guided by 90th-percentile runtime estimates is \LearnedGainMinPct{}--\LearnedGainMaxPct\% cheaper than delay on Philly under our default cap curve and by at most \AcmeLearnedMixGainMaxPct\% on Acme, where exact runtimes would save \AcmeOracleMixGainPctZero\%; better accuracy did not lower the cost, so predictors for such controllers should be judged by the decisions they lead to. Reading predictions naively for long-running jobs made the controller \ContractOverHoldMin{} to \ContractOverHoldMax{} times more expensive.
