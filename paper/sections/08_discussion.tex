\section{Discussion, Limitations and Conclusion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\emph{When is offloading worth using?} In our runs, bursting lowered the cost only when the controller knew the exact remaining runtime of each job, and mainly in 4-hour events with 20--30\% cuts. It also needs enough cloud capacity for the cut: with \SetupCloudCapGpus{} cloud GPUs, burst-only did not comply in any event with a 30\% cut. A lower cloud price, such as spot capacity, would change this balance; this paper does not evaluate it.

\emph{Limitations.} We use one trace and one month of test data. The simulator has no virtual-cluster quotas, placement constraints or start-up delay, so its waits differ from the trace (Section~\ref{sec:method}). The GPU pool is constant. The transformer was trained with one seed. Jobs submitted in the training period appear at test events; masking their predictions moved the median ratio of the p90 mix to delay-only by at most \LearnedMaskShiftMax{} for each of the three learned predictors (Section~\ref{sec:eval}). Host power, the cap curve and the migration and restore times are assumptions (Table~\ref{tab:params}). Burst jobs never return on premise. The carbon check uses annual average intensities and event-window energy only. The cloud price comes from a listing site.

\emph{Conclusion.} Delay is the cheapest single way for a GPU cluster to cut power on request; capping alone falls short and bursting alone costs a median of \BurstOverDelayDefaultOne{} to \BurstOverDelayDefaultZero{} times as much as delay per event in the default cell. Guided by 90th-percentile runtime estimates, a controller that combines delay and capping is a median of \LearnedGainMinPct{} to \LearnedGainMaxPct\% cheaper per event than delay alone, depending on the learned predictor, and offloading adds almost nothing to that gain. Bursting helped only with exact runtimes in long, deep events, and reading predictions naively for long-running jobs made the controller \ContractOverHoldMin{} to \ContractOverHoldMax{} times more expensive.
