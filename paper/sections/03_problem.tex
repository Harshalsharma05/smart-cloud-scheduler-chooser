\section{Problem Formulation}
\label{sec:problem}
We model a GPU cluster that receives a request to reduce its electrical load for a limited time, and ask what the cheapest way to comply is.
The cluster is a pool of $G$ GPUs. A job $j$ needs $g_j$ GPUs, has a mean GPU utilisation $u_j$, a wall-clock runtime $w_j$ and ends with a status in $\{\textit{Pass}, \textit{Failed}, \textit{Killed}\}$.

\subsection{Grid event and target}
A grid event $\varepsilon=(t_s, D, \kappa)$ asks the cluster to cut its power by a fraction $\kappa$ during $[t_s, t_s{+}D)$, in line with the demand-response setting~\cite{liu13,zhou24}.
Let $P_0(t)$ be the facility power if the cluster takes no action (the baseline). The target is one flat level,
\begin{equation}
P^\star=(1-\kappa)\,\bar P_0,\qquad \bar P_0=\tfrac{1}{D}\int_{t_s}^{t_s+D}P_0(t)\,dt .
\end{equation}
A policy is judged against the baseline from the same starting state (a paired run). Its shed energy is
\begin{equation}
E_{\text{shed}}=\int_{t_s}^{t_s+D}\bigl(P_0(t)-P(t)\bigr)\,dt ,
\end{equation}
and its compliance is the fraction of the event during which $P(t)$ stays within a small tolerance of $P^\star$.

\subsection{Facility power}
A busy GPU running job $j$ under a power cap level $f\in(0,1]$ (a fraction of the maximum GPU power $P_{\max}$) draws
\begin{equation}
P_{\text{gpu}}(u_j,f)=\min\bigl(P_{\text{idle}}+u_j(P_{\max}-P_{\text{idle}}),\; f\,P_{\max}\bigr),
\end{equation}
where $P_{\text{idle}}$ is the idle GPU power. With $N_b$ busy and $N_i=G-N_b$ idle on-premise GPUs, the facility power is
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
P(t)=\text{PUE}\Bigl[&\sum_{j\in\text{running}} g_j\,P_{\text{gpu}}(u_j,f)+N_i P_{\text{idle}}\\
&+G\,H_{\text{idle}}+N_b H_{\text{busy}}\Bigr],
\end{split}
\end{equation}
with the power usage effectiveness PUE, a host power $H_{\text{idle}}$ per installed GPU, and an extra host power $H_{\text{busy}}$ per busy GPU. The cap does not change host power. Power is constant between simulator events, so energies are exact.
Even with every job removed, the cluster draws the idle floor $P_{\text{floor}}=\text{PUE}\cdot G\,(P_{\text{idle}}+H_{\text{idle}})$. An event with $P^\star<P_{\text{floor}}$ cannot be met by any policy; we call it \emph{infeasible} and exclude it from all results.

\subsection{Actions}
At the event start the cluster may use three actions on running jobs; all other jobs are scheduled first-come first-served with skip-ahead and no runtime estimates.
\emph{Suspend.} The job's GPUs are freed at once and its progress is kept. Suspended jobs resume when the event ends, in submission order, each paying a restore overhead $\tau_r$ of extra work. Any new job that would push power above $P^\star$ is held in the queue until the event ends (admission gating); held jobs count as delay.
\emph{Cap.} A cluster-wide level $f$ from a small discrete set applies to all on-premise GPUs. A job whose uncapped power is $P_{\text{gpu}}(u_j,1)$ runs at speed $s_j(f)=1$ if $r_j=fP_{\max}/P_{\text{gpu}}(u_j,1)\ge 1$, and otherwise at $s_j(f)=\sigma(r_j)$, an assumed piecewise-linear curve (Table~\ref{tab:params}) anchored by one published measurement of about 22\% less server power for about 10\% slower work~\cite{patel24}; capping has also been measured at HPC scale~\cite{zhao23}. A slower job finishes later, and power falls only through the job's lower GPU draw.
\emph{Burst.} The job is checkpointed and its on-premise GPUs are freed at once. A cloud instance with $g_j$ GPUs is requested, boots for $\tau_b$, then finishes the job at full speed after an extra migration overhead $\tau_m$ (checkpoint transfer and warm-up). The job never returns on premise. The cloud bills $\pi$ per GPU-hour from the moment the instance is ready until it is released, with a minimum billed time, and at most $C_{\max}$ cloud GPUs may be in use at once. Boot, checkpoint and restore overheads follow measured ranges~\cite{mao12,mohan21}.

\subsection{Cost of an event}
Let $T_j$ and $T_j^0$ be the finish time of job $j$ under the policy and in the baseline, and $\delta_j=\max(0,T_j-T_j^0)/3600$ its delay in hours. Delay from suspension, capping, gating and cloud boot is measured the same way. With $c_d$ the cost of a delayed GPU-hour, the cost of a policy is
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
C=C_{\text{cloud}}&+c_d\!\!\sum_{j\in\text{Pass}}\! g_j\delta_j+w_f c_d\!\!\sum_{j\in\text{Fail/Kill}}\! g_j\delta_j\\
&+m\,c_d\!\!\sum_{j\in\mathcal M}\! g_j\,\tfrac{w_j}{3600},
\end{split}
\end{equation}
where $C_{\text{cloud}}$ is the cloud bill, $w_f$ is the weight of delay on jobs that fail or are killed, and $\mathcal M$ is the set of jobs with a deadline that they meet in the baseline and miss under the policy, each charged $m$ re-runs. We set $c_d$ equal to the cloud price $\pi$, so that an hour of delay is worth an hour of rented GPU. Delaying work that will fail or be cancelled loses no useful output, so the primary setting is $w_f=0$ (cloud money spent on such jobs is always counted); we also report $w_f=1$. The headline metric is
\begin{equation}
\text{USD/MWh}=C/E_{\text{shed}}\quad\text{if }E_{\text{shed}}\ge 0.1\,\kappa\,\bar P_0 D,\label{eq:usdmwh}
\end{equation}
and undefined otherwise, so a policy that barely sheds does not look cheap. A policy whose compliance is below $99\%$ is never reported as a winner.

\begin{center}
\fbox{\begin{minipage}{0.94\columnwidth}\small
\textbf{Assumptions.}
(i) \emph{Job timing.} A job holds its GPUs continuously for its wall-clock time from first start to final end; retry gaps and attempts are merged, because the predictors are defined on this clock.
(ii) \emph{Checkpoints.} Suspension is instantaneous; migration and restore cost a constant number of extra seconds.
(iii) \emph{Deadlines.} A seeded random $30\%$ of jobs get a deadline equal to submission plus a slack factor drawn from $U(2,4)$ times the job's wall time, independent of its status.
(iv) \emph{Pool.} The pool $G$ is constant (no failures or maintenance), with one GPU type and trace-derived utilisation.
(v) \emph{Controller.} Actions are planned once at the event start, with no re-planning.
\end{minipage}}
\end{center}
