\section{Methodology}
\label{sec:method}

\subsection{Trace and split}
We use the job log of the Microsoft Philly trace~\cite{jeon19}. Jobs are split by submission time (UTC): training before 1~November 2017, validation from 1 to 14~November, and test from 15~November to 15~December. The predictors are trained and tuned on the first two periods only.
The simulation starts on 8~November, so the seven days before the test window are a warm-up that fills the queue and the pool. It replays \MethSimJobs{} jobs, of which \MethWarmupJobs{} are warm-up jobs. Jobs already running on 8~November enter at time zero with their elapsed time (\MethCarryJobs{} carry-over jobs on \MethCarryGpus{} GPUs).
Baseline statistics count only the \MotTestJobs{} test jobs. The cost of a policy is the paired difference over all jobs.

\subsection{Pool and baseline check}
The machine list gives \SetupInstalledGpus{} installed GPUs, derived from the utilisation records. In the test window only about \SetupPoolGpus{} of them report a utilisation value in a typical minute. We treat the others as unavailable and simulate a pool of \SetupPoolGpus{} GPUs, the median number of reporting GPUs.
Table~\ref{tab:baseline} compares the simulated baseline with the trace. Busy GPUs and utilisation match. Waits do not: the simulated mean wait is shorter and its 90th percentile is longer. The simulator has no virtual-cluster quotas, no placement constraints and no start-up delay. We did not tune it to match the waits.

\subsection{Grid events}
An event lasts 1, 2 or 4~hours and asks for a cut of 10, 20 or 30\%. All nine combinations share the same \MethEventDays{} start times. A fixed seed draws \MethEventDays{} distinct days between \MethEventFirstDate{} and \MethEventLastDate{} (at least two days after the start of the test window and ending at least one day before its end), and one half-hour slot per day from \MethEventFirstStartHhmm{} to \MethEventLastStartHhmm{} Pacific time.
This gives \SetupEventsTotal{} events. In \SetupEventsInfeasible{} of them, all with a \SetupInfeasibleCutPct\% cut, the target is below the idle floor $P_{\text{floor}}$. We exclude them, which leaves \SetupEventsFeasible{} events. The default cell is 2~hours and 20\%.
Each event is a paired run: the baseline and every policy start from the same snapshot at $t_s$ and run until 24~hours after the event ends.

\subsection{Predictors and policies}
We compare five predictors (Section~\ref{sec:design}): the last-two-jobs average~\cite{tsafrir07}, gradient-boosted trees, LightGBM~\cite{ke17}, the transformer, and the oracle. Each writes rows on the elapsed-time grid.
\MethNoPredJobs{} simulated jobs, \MethNoPredCarryJobs{} of them carry-over jobs, have no prediction row and use their true values. Because these jobs run long, they hold a median of \MethNoPredGpuShareMedianPct\% of the running GPUs at an event start; Section~\ref{sec:eval} shows that this fallback does not change the results.
Warm-up and carry-over jobs were submitted in the training or validation period, so their predictions are in-sample for the learned models.
The policies are delay-only, cap-only, burst-only, the mix with the median or the 90th-percentile cloud bill, and mix-nocloud: the same mix with no cloud capacity ($C_{\max}=0$), which separates the effect of bursting from that of capping and job choice. The hold-beyond-grid lookup rule is the default; the contract rule is a robustness check.

\subsection{Parameters and metrics}
Table~\ref{tab:params} lists the main parameters. The maximum GPU power is that of the P100\footnote{NVIDIA Tesla P100 PCIe datasheet, \url{https://nvidia.com/content/dam/en-zz/Solutions/Data-Center/tesla-p100/pdf/nvidia-tesla-p100-PCIe-datasheet.pdf}, accessed 13 November 2026. The trace names no GPU model.}, the PUE is the industry average of a survey\footnote{Uptime Institute Global Data Center Survey 2023, \url{https://journal.uptimeinstitute.com/large-data-centers-are-mostly-more-efficient-analysis-confirms/}, accessed 13 November 2026.}, and the cloud price is the on-demand price of a one-GPU instance\footnote{AWS EC2 p3.2xlarge (one V100), on-demand, us-east-1, as listed at \url{https://instances.vantage.sh/aws/ec2/p3.2xlarge}, accessed 13 November 2026.}.
For each event and policy we compute the cost in dollars per MWh shed (Eq.~\ref{eq:usdmwh}). A policy can win a cell only if its compliance is at least 99\%. The primary cost uses $w_f=0$; we also report $w_f=1$. To compare policies we divide each policy's cost by the delay-only cost of the same event and report the median of this ratio over events. All runs use one seed.
