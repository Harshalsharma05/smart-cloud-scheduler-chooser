\begin{abstract}
% Budget: 0.3 pages with the title, at most 200 words. Written in W5 (abstract v1 due 24 Nov AoE); only numbers that exist.
Power grids increasingly ask large consumers to cut load at evening peaks, and GPU clusters are large, fast-growing loads. A cluster can respond by delaying jobs, capping GPU power, or bursting running jobs to rented cloud GPUs. Offloading as a priced grid action is understudied. We compare the three in dollars per MWh shed, using trace-driven simulation of the Microsoft Philly cluster with events of 1--4 hours and 10--30\% cuts, and build a controller that mixes them and chooses jobs using predicted remaining runtime and failure risk from a small transformer over each user's job history. Delaying jobs is the cheapest single action (roughly \$3k--13k per MWh for a 2-hour, 20\% cut). Power capping alone reaches less than half of a 20\% cut, and bursting alone costs 30 to 125 times more, because the cloud bill covers a job's whole remaining runtime. A mixed controller that budgets bursts with the transformer's 90th-percentile runtime estimate is a few percent cheaper than delay alone in both cost weightings, and a naive reading of runtime predictions for long-running jobs makes the controller 10 to 70 times more expensive. We discuss when offloading is worth using.
\end{abstract}
