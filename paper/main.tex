\begin{abstract}
% Budget: 0.3 pages with the title, at most 200 words. Written in W5 (abstract v1 due 24 Nov AoE); only numbers that exist.
Grids ask large consumers, such as GPU clusters, to cut peak load. A cluster can delay jobs, cap GPU power or burst jobs to a public cloud, yet offloading as a priced grid action remains understudied. We price the three in dollars per MWh shed on two production traces, a 2017 deep-learning cluster (Microsoft Philly) and a 2023 LLM-development cluster (Acme), for events of 1--4 hours and 10--30\% cuts, with a controller that selects jobs using learned remaining-runtime and failure-risk predictions. Delay is the cheapest action that meets the cut on both traces. For a 2-hour, 20\% cut, bursting alone costs a median of 125 (Philly) and 12 (Acme) times as much as delay per event when delaying failed work costs nothing, since the cloud bill covers a job's whole remaining runtime and long jobs worsen bursting. With the transformer predictor, it beat delay in at most one of 174 events; a cheaper cloud does not change this. The predictor-guided mix outperforms delay by 5--16\% on Philly under our default cap curve and by at most 1.5\% on Acme, where exact runtimes would save 17\%. Reading predictions naively for long-running jobs makes the controller 11 to 68 times more expensive.
\end{abstract}
