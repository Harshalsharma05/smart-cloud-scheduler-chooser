\begin{abstract}
% Budget: 0.3 pages with the title, at most 200 words. Written in W5 (abstract v1 due 24 Nov AoE); only numbers that exist.
Grids increasingly ask large consumers, including GPU clusters, to cut evening-peak load. A cluster can delay jobs, cap GPU power, or burst jobs to the cloud. Offloading as a priced action is understudied. We compare the three in dollars per MWh shed, using trace-driven simulation of the Microsoft Philly cluster with events of 1--4 hours and 10--30\% cuts, and build a controller that mixes them and chooses jobs using predicted remaining runtime and failure risk from a small transformer over each user's job history. Delay is the cheapest single action (roughly \$3k--13k per MWh for a 2-hour, 20\% cut). In that cell, power capping alone reaches less than half of the cut, and bursting alone costs 30 to 125 times more, because the cloud bill covers a job's whole remaining runtime. A controller combining delay and capping, guided by the transformer's 90th-percentile runtime estimate, is about 5\% cheaper than delay alone in both cost weightings, yet offloading adds almost nothing to that gain; bursting helps only with near-perfect runtime knowledge, in long, deep events. A naive reading of runtime predictions for long-running jobs makes the controller 10 to 70 times more expensive. We discuss when offloading is worth using.
\end{abstract}
