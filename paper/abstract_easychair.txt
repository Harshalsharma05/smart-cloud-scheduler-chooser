Grids ask large consumers, such as GPU clusters, to cut peak load. A cluster can delay jobs, cap GPU power, or burst jobs to a public cloud; offloading as a priced grid action is understudied. We price the three in dollars per MWh shed on two production traces, a 2017 deep-learning cluster (Microsoft Philly) and a 2023 LLM-development cluster (Acme), for events of 1–4 hours and 10–30% cuts, with a controller that chooses jobs using learned remaining-runtime and failure-risk predictions. Delay is the cheapest action that meets the cut on both traces. For a 2-hour, 20% cut, bursting alone costs a median of 125 (Philly) and 12 (Acme) times as much as delay per event when delaying failed work costs nothing; the cloud bill covers a job's whole remaining runtime, so long jobs make bursting much worse. With the transformer predictor it beat delay in at most one of 174 events, and a cheaper cloud does not change this. The predictor-guided mix beats delay by 5–16% on Philly under our default cap curve and not at all on Acme, where exact runtimes would save 17%. Reading predictions naively for long-running jobs makes the controller 11 to 68 times more expensive.
